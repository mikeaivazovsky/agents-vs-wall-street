Reading the filings, then doing the arithmetic

Agents vs Wall Street · 16 August 2026 · four companies, twelve figures, one command

The guiding rule of this system is a division of labour: deterministic code where the data is structured, a model only where it is not. Financial statements are tables, and a parser reads a table more cheaply, more repeatably and more accurately than a language model does. Management commentary is prose, and there a model earns its place.

Everything below is what runs today. Where a stage is designed but not built, it says so.

How a figure is produced

forecast.py -> pipeline -> gather (history) -> history_analyzer -> llm_adjuster -> metric_forecaster -> writer

Why the split is where it is

TaskDone byWhy
Select candidate documentsPythonFront matter is machine readable
Read a financial statement tablePythonThe data is a grid; a parser is exact, free and repeatable
Read scale, period and reporting spanPythonAll three are printed in or above the table
Reject an impossible figurePythonA unit constrains what it can hold
Extrapolate the next periodPythonSee the note on research below
Judge whether the extrapolation is fairModelRequires reading a cycle, not a cell
Read guidance out of prose (designed)ModelThere is no structure to parse

The research is why the arithmetic anchors and the model corrects, not the reverse. GPT-4 forecasts of earnings carry larger errors than analyst consensus and lean optimistic. On quarterly earnings per share, seasonal extrapolation and a fine-tuned time series model showed the lowest errors, and analysts beat seasonal extrapolation only slightly more often than not. This competition scores our miss against Wall Street's miss, so a forecast produced by a model alone would be expected to lose by construction.

What the extraction stage actually had to solve

The corpus converts published documents to markdown, and the conversion is lossy in ways that quietly corrupt figures. Each of these was found by reading the extractor's own output, and each is now a rule.

Three levels of trust, and why they exist

A figure that parses cleanly is not therefore correct. Trust is assigned by comparing documents against each other, because the same figure is printed many times and independent conversions agreeing is the strongest evidence available here.

LevelMeaning
confirmedNothing contradicts it, and either the reading raised no concern or separate documents agree on it
conflictedAnother document states a different figure for the same period, so at least one reading is wrong
doubtfulNothing contradicts it, but a concern was recorded and no second document backs it up

Only confirmed figures reach the forecast. This was tested rather than assumed. Of the doubtful figures that could be checked against a confirmed one for the same period, 0 of 78 agreed. They are typically real numbers carrying the wrong period, an annual total sitting in a quarterly series, which destroys both the trend and the seasonal shape it is meant to inform.

From history to a figure

Two kinds of metric behave differently and are treated differently.

  • Flows - revenue, profit, earnings per share - compound. They are carried forward by multiplying the same period a year earlier by the median growth of recent periods. The comparison is year on year rather than quarter on quarter because these businesses are seasonal: Home Depot sells most in spring, Deere in the planting season.
  • Ratios - gross margin, comparable sales - do not compound. Doubling revenue does not double a margin. They are carried from their recent level by the amount they have typically moved.

The median is used rather than the mean so that one exceptional quarter does not set the rate for the next.

Combining signals when some are missing

guidance 0.6   management is describing a period already trading
history  0.4   extrapolation only knows the past

weights are normalised over whichever signals exist,
so a lone signal takes the full weight

Commentary does not produce a figure of its own. It produces a proportional correction, capped at ten per cent, applied after the reported evidence has spoken. A missing signal lowers the support behind an answer and is named in the run record, but never stops a figure being produced: an empty cell scores the maximum penalty.

Guarding against a biased model

These are the failure modes Iexpect from putting a language model near a forecast, and what the design does about each.

RiskMitigationState
Memorisation leakage. The target periods are unreported, but the model may recall news, comment or third-party forecastsThe prompt states that the model has no knowledge after the last period shown and must not use recollection of the companyIn place
Anchoring on guidance. Once guidance sits in the prompt the model stays near it whatever the evidenceGuidance is weighed arithmetically outside the model and never placed in its promptBy construction
Narrative tone contamination. Management comment is optimistic by construction, and a model reading a transcript inherits its sentiment; text can also be read as instruction The model sees only the numeric series, never the prose, and its correction is cappedIn place
Coverage asymmetry. Training data holds far more about Home Depot than about Hays, reproducing inside the model the coverage gap the challenge attributes to human analysts Identical prompt, identical series length and identical bounds for all four companiesIn place
Prompt framing. Wording becomes part of the forecast One fixed prompt, temperature zero, answers cached by hash so a rerun cannot driftIn place
Obliging behaviour. Asked to review, a model finds something to change The prompt states that zero is a correct and expected answer Insufficient - see weaknesses

Reliability of the run

  • Each company is processed independently. A failure on the third does not lose the first two.
  • The history is written to artifacts/ before any figure is decided from it, so the record of what the run had to work with survives a later failure.
  • Model answers are cached by a hash covering the model, the prompt and the input, so a rerun in the final window costs nothing and returns the same figures.
  • An unreachable model returns nothing and the decision falls back to arithmetic. No figure depends on a call succeeding.
  • Units are a type, not a convention. The writer refuses a figure whose unit does not match the specification for the cell, and reads back the label and unit printed beside that cell before writing.
  • The whole run takes about thirty seconds and reads 1 139 documents.

What is weak, and what Iwould fix next

Four of the twelve figures rest on history Iknow is misread. The extractor takes a row whose label matches but whose meaning does not: for Hays it captures a division rather than the group, for Analog Devices' gross margin a dollar line rather than the percentage, for Deere's earnings per share an annual figure rather than a quarter. The citations show this plainly, because the trail leads to the wrong row and the wrong row is visible. The forecast then extrapolates it faithfully. The arithmetic is not the problem; the input is.

The model's sign does not always follow its own reasoning. On Home Depot's adjusted earnings per share it argued that declines had steepened and that the extrapolation was too optimistic, then returned a correction of plus three per cent. The schema does not tie the direction to the argument. The fix is a separate direction field checked against the sign, and a second independent answer required to agree before any correction is applied.

The model almost never answers zero. Across twelve metrics it declined to adjust once. The rest cluster between five and eight per cent, near the cap. The prompt says that zero is expected; that is evidently not enough on its own.

Also unfinished, in the order Iwould take them:

  1. Guidance. The strongest missing signal. Management states what it expects for the very period being forecast. The language is narrow enough to filter cheaply: 159 candidate paragraphs across all four companies once documents older than the last reported quarter are dropped, about 28 000 tokens.
  2. Side factors - demand, pricing, input costs, rates, regulation - read from call transcripts, which contain no tables at all and are therefore genuinely a model's job.
  3. Repair the four broken series, most usefully by requiring a group total rather than a division, and by pinning the reporting basis to the row label.

What Itried and changed

  • A hand written plausible range per metric was removed. It looked like validation, but the bounds were invented, which is worse than no check at all. What replaced it states only what a unit can physically hold, and company-specific bounds will be measured from history instead.
  • Widening the search for dates raised apparent confidence from 48 to 62 per cent, and the gain was false. Dates taken from beside a table were attached to whichever figure came first. The cross-document check exposed it, and a date from anywhere but the column itself is now marked unsettled.
  • The specification was cut back to hold only what the organisers' own companies.json does not state: the sheet and the cell. Labels, units, periods and file names are read from their file, so the two cannot drift apart.
  • Placeholders were made obviously wrong on purpose. While the forecaster was being built the cells held zero and the run exited non-zero, because a plausible looking placeholder can be uploaded by mistake and a zero cannot.

Running it

poetry install
poetry run python forecast.py

One command produces all four workbooks in submission/, four history files in artifacts/ and one timestamped log in logs/. It exits non-zero if any workbook is missing or if any figure is still a placeholder. Uploads to OpenStocks are manual; nothing in this repository submits anything.

Results justification can be found in logs/. Just open log file and search for ADI-revenew, for example.

Python 3.12 under poetry, with openpyxl, pydantic, pyyaml and openai. Node is present only to run the organisers' submission checks.
